COUNTERPARTY EXPOSURE RISK POLICY
Document ID: RP-2024-011 (Synthetic/Fictional — for demonstration purposes only)
Effective Date: January 15, 2024
Owner: Enterprise Risk Management Division

1. PURPOSE
This policy establishes the framework for measuring, monitoring, and limiting counterparty
credit exposure across all trading and lending desks at Meridian Fictional Bank ("the Bank").
Counterparty exposure risk arises when a trading partner, borrower, or issuer fails to meet
its contractual obligations, resulting in financial loss to the Bank.

2. EXPOSURE LIMITS
No single counterparty rated investment-grade (BBB- or above) may account for more than 8% of
the Bank's Tier 1 capital in aggregate exposure. Counterparties rated below investment-grade
are capped at 2% of Tier 1 capital. Exposure is calculated using current mark-to-market value
plus a potential future exposure (PFE) add-on determined by asset class and maturity.

3. CONCENTRATION THRESHOLDS
Aggregate exposure to any single industry sector shall not exceed 25% of the total counterparty
credit portfolio. Aggregate exposure to counterparties domiciled in a single non-OECD jurisdiction
shall not exceed 10% of the total portfolio. Breaches must be reported to the Chief Risk Officer
within one business day.

4. NETTING AND COLLATERAL
Exposure calculations may incorporate legally enforceable netting agreements (ISDA Master
Agreements with a Credit Support Annex) and eligible collateral, subject to a minimum haircut
of 2% for cash and 8% for investment-grade government securities. Collateral must be
revalued daily.

5. MONITORING AND ESCALATION
The Counterparty Risk Committee reviews aggregate exposures weekly. Any counterparty exceeding
90% of its assigned limit triggers an amber alert and mandatory review; exceeding 100% triggers
a red alert, immediate trading freeze with that counterparty, and escalation to the CRO and
Board Risk Committee within 24 hours.

6. STRESS TESTING
Counterparty exposures are stress-tested quarterly against a minimum of three scenarios: a
sudden two-notch downgrade of the largest 10 counterparties, a 30% market value shock to
derivative positions, and a correlated default of the top 3 counterparties in a single sector.

7. REVIEW CYCLE
This policy is reviewed annually by Enterprise Risk Management and approved by the Board Risk
Committee. Exceptions require written CRO sign-off and expire after 90 days unless renewed.
